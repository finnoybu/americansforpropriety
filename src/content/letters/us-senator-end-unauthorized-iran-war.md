**Dear Senator {{rep_last_name}},**

I'm writing as a constituent in {{my_city}}, {{my_state}} to ask you to vote to end US hostilities against Iran unless and until Congress authorizes them, and to make that a priority when the new Senate convenes in January.

The Constitution gives Congress, not the President, the power to take the country to war. Congress never authorized military action against Iran. The War Powers Resolution gave the President 60 days to obtain that authorization after the strikes began on February 28; that deadline passed on May 1. The administration's answer was to tell Congress the hostilities had "terminated" because of the ceasefire, while US forces kept up a naval blockade of Iranian ports. Under long-standing international law, a blockade is an act of war. A ceasefire that can collapse at any time is not a peace.

In June, Congress passed an Iran war powers measure for the first time. That was the right instinct. The new Senate should finish the job.

I'm asking you to:

1. **Vote for every Iran war powers resolution** that comes before the Senate, and support bringing one up early in the 120th Congress.
2. **Oppose further war funding** that is not tied to a congressional authorization, including supplemental requests sent outside the regular appropriations process.
3. **Demand a public accounting** of the war's costs, casualties, and legal basis, through hearings in the Foreign Relations and Armed Services committees.
