> **Who this is for.** Only senators in the Democratic caucus vote for Majority Leader. That includes independents who caucus with Democrats, and it includes the senators-elect who won on November 3 and take office in January. If both of your senators are Republicans, they don't get a vote. Send this to a Democratic senator-elect from your state instead, through their campaign or transition contact, if you have one.
>
> **Where things stand (November 8, 2026).** Democrats will hold somewhere between 54 and 56 seats in January. Chuck Schumer says he will be re-elected leader, and reporting says he has the votes. At least five incoming senators said during their campaigns that they would not back him. The ballot is secret and a simple majority wins. A letter backing someone other than the front-runner is still worth sending: a caucus that hears a large number of constituents asking for different leadership has to account for that, whoever wins.
>
> **Personalize** the paragraph about your choice. One or two concrete reasons in your own words carry more weight than a list. If you kept the suggested choice, see the example paragraph below.

---

**Dear Senator {{rep_last_name}},**

I'm writing as a constituent in {{my_city}}, {{my_state}} about the vote you will cast for Senate Democratic Leader. Because Democrats won the majority on November 3, whoever wins that vote becomes Senate Majority Leader in January. I'm asking you to vote for **{{leader_choice}}**.

Voters gave this caucus a large majority after two years of rising prices, a war in Iran that Congress never authorized, and an administration that treats the limits on its power as optional. The leader you choose will decide which bills reach the floor, how hard the Senate pushes back on unlawful executive action, how nominees are handled, and whether the majority spends the next two years running out the clock or delivering.

I want a Majority Leader who will:

1. Bring the affordability agenda voters asked for to the floor quickly, starting with health care costs and the Medicaid cuts in the 2025 reconciliation law.
2. Use the Senate's full authority to reassert Congress's role over war, tariffs, and spending.
3. Be willing to change Senate rules when a minority uses them to block bills with majority support.
4. Treat the oversight power as a duty, not a bargaining chip.

[Explain why {{leader_choice}} is the right person for the job. Use your own words. Example if you kept the suggested choice: "Senator Sanders has spent decades on the issues this election turned on: the cost of health care, wages, and the influence of concentrated money in politics. He chaired the Budget Committee and the HELP Committee, so he knows how to move legislation through the Senate, and he has been clear about what he would do with a majority."]

I know this is a secret ballot and that you will weigh many things. I'm asking you to weigh this too: your constituents are paying attention to who leads the majority we just elected.

I'd appreciate knowing how you plan to vote, or at least what you are looking for in a leader.

Thank you for representing our state.

Sincerely,

**[Your name]**
[Your full address]
