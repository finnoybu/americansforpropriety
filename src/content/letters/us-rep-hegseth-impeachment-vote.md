> **Why now.** Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) introduced eight articles of impeachment on September 15 and read them on the House floor, but the House left before a vote. He has said he will bring it back as a privileged resolution in the first week of the lame-duck session, which opens the week of November 9. Once the parliamentarian agrees it qualifies, the House must act on it within two legislative days. Leaders can try to avoid a direct vote with a motion to table, so the tabling vote may be the one that matters.
>
> **What the articles charge.** Three concern the Iran war: continuing hostilities without authorization, past the War Powers Resolution's deadlines, and after Congress voted to end them. Others charge that the Secretary ignored legal protections for civilians (centered on the February 28 strike on a school in Minab, Iran), that strikes on alleged drug-smuggling boats were extrajudicial killings, and that the seizure of Nicolás Maduro in Venezuela and hostilities in Yemen lacked legal authority. One concerns alleged retaliation against Sen. Mark Kelly for statements to service members.
>
> **The other side.** Hegseth has called the articles a sham brought by a defeated congressman, and the President has said Hegseth is doing a very good job. A Senate conviction requires two-thirds of senators present, so removal is unlikely in this Congress. A House vote still puts every member on record about whether Congress's war powers mean anything.
>
> **Personalize.** If your representative has voted for Iran war powers resolutions, point out that this vote is the same principle with consequences attached. If they have voted against, ask what authority they believe the Pentagon has been acting under.

I'm writing as a constituent in {{my_city}}, {{my_state}} to ask you to vote against any motion to table or set aside the articles of impeachment against Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, and to vote yes on impeachment when it comes to the floor.

The Constitution gives Congress, not the executive branch, the power to take the country to war. Congress never authorized the war with Iran that began on February 28. The War Powers Resolution's 60-day clock ran out on May 1, and in June Congress itself voted to stop the hostilities. The articles Rep. Massie has filed charge that the Secretary kept those operations going anyway, and also charge that lethal strikes on boats in the Caribbean and Eastern Pacific amounted to extrajudicial killings.

Impeachment is the tool the Constitution gives the House to hold executive officers accountable for serious abuses of office. If Congress declines to use it when it believes its war powers have been ignored, it tells every future administration that those limits are optional.

I'm asking you to:

1. **Vote no on any motion to table or refer** the impeachment resolution. Members should cast a direct vote on the merits.
2. **Vote yes on the articles of impeachment,** especially those concerning hostilities waged without congressional authorization.
3. **Support hearings in the new Congress** on the legal basis, costs, and civilian casualties of the Iran war and the boat strikes, whatever the outcome of this vote.
