> **Personalize** with your own numbers if you can: what your marketplace premium was in 2025 versus 2026, or how a Medicaid paperwork requirement has affected you or someone you know. One specific dollar figure is worth more than a paragraph of policy.
>
> **If your representative is a Democrat,** the ask is speed and no watering down: these bills should move in the first weeks. **If your representative is a Republican,** the ask is a yes vote. Health costs were a top concern for voters in both parties this year.
>
> **Send to your representative-elect as well** if your district elected someone new on November 3.

---

**Dear Representative {{rep_last_name}},**

I'm writing as a constituent in {{my_city}}, {{my_state}} to ask you to vote, early in the new Congress, to restore the enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits and to reverse the Medicaid cuts enacted in the 2025 reconciliation law.

The enhanced premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025. Millions of people who buy coverage on the ACA marketplaces have paid sharply higher premiums this year as a result, and some have dropped coverage they could no longer afford. At the same time, the 2025 law's Medicaid cuts are phasing in. Coverage for many lawfully present immigrants has already been cut, and new work and paperwork requirements are set to start at the beginning of 2027. Wherever those requirements have been tried, they have pushed eligible people off coverage over paperwork.

I'm asking you to:

1. **Restore the enhanced ACA premium tax credits** in time to lower 2027 premiums, and make them permanent so families aren't facing the same cliff again in two years.
2. **Repeal the Medicaid work and paperwork requirements** before they take effect, restore coverage for the lawfully present immigrants the law cut off, and restore the federal funding the 2025 law cut from state Medicaid programs.
3. **Move these bills first.** Voters were told health costs would be a day-one priority. They should see a floor vote in the first weeks of the 120th Congress, not the last.

I know any bill still has to get through the Senate and past a possible veto. That is a reason to pass it quickly and clearly, so that the public can see who stands where.

I'd appreciate knowing your position.

Thank you for representing our district.
