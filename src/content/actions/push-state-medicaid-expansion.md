Ten states have not adopted Medicaid expansion under the Affordable Care Act: Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kansas, Mississippi, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Wisconsin, and Wyoming (status varies by year as some states inch toward partial coverage).

The roughly 4 million Americans in the resulting coverage gap earn too much for traditional Medicaid in their state but too little for ACA marketplace subsidies. They are workers — most are in low-wage jobs in retail, food service, agriculture, construction. They have no pathway to coverage at any price they can afford.

State-level Medicaid expansion fights are different from federal policy fights. State legislatures and governors decide. Where the legislature has refused, ballot measures have succeeded — Maine, Idaho, Missouri, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Utah, Nebraska have all passed expansion through ballot measures after legislative refusal.

If you live in a non-expansion state, your state legislators and governor are the targets. Letters that connect expansion to specific local outcomes (rural hospital survival, your county's uninsured rate, a personal story) carry more weight than abstract policy arguments. The empirical case is strong; the political case is what needs reinforcement.
