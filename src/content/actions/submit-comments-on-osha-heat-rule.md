OSHA has been working on a federal heat injury and illness prevention standard for years. The standard would set water, rest, and shade requirements for outdoor and indoor workers exposed to high heat, and would require acclimatization protocols for new workers and after extended absences.

Federal rules like this are made through a public-comment process. Comments from individual workers, small businesses, and constituents — especially those describing specific workplaces or experiences — are read and counted. They shape the final rule's scope, exemptions, and effective dates.

A useful comment is short, specific, and grounded in something you know. Three or four paragraphs is enough. The format expectations are minimal — no legalese required. The deadline for comments is typically published 60-90 days after a proposed rule appears in the Federal Register.

If you work in agriculture, construction, warehousing, food service, kitchens, or any job that exposes you to heat — your comment carries particular weight.
