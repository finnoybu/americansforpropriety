OSHA spent years building a federal heat injury and illness prevention standard. The proposed rule would set water, rest, and shade requirements for outdoor and indoor workers exposed to high heat, and would require acclimatization protocols for new workers and after extended absences.

The rule has been through the full process. OSHA published the proposed rule in August 2024, the public comment period closed in January 2025, the informal public hearing concluded in July 2025, and the post-hearing comment period closed at the end of October 2025. The dockets are closed — submitting a comment is no longer the action available. The record is complete.

What is missing is a final-action date. The proposed rule is stalled, and the most recent regulatory agenda set no timeline for finalizing it. Until it does, there is no enforceable federal heat standard — only a National Emphasis Program that directs inspections and can be revised or left to lapse, as the heat NEP did in April 2026 before being replaced.

Letters to OSHA and the Department of Labor urging them to finalize the rule — without further delay and without weakening it — are read by the offices responsible for moving it. If you work in agriculture, construction, warehousing, food service, kitchens, or any job that exposes you to heat, your letter carries particular weight.
