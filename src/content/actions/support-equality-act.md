The Supreme Court's 2020 *Bostock v. Clayton County* ruling held that Title VII's prohibition on sex discrimination in employment includes sexual orientation and gender identity. The 2022 Respect for Marriage Act gave federal statutory protection to same-sex and interracial marriages.

Important wins. Also incomplete.

The Fair Housing Act's federal protections do not, as a matter of statutory text, cover sexual orientation or gender identity. Many federally funded programs (education, healthcare, public accommodations) operate under similar gaps. Many states do fill the gap; many do not.

The Equality Act would close those gaps systematically. It would amend federal civil rights statutes — Title VII, Title IX, the Fair Housing Act, the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, federal jury service, and federally funded programs broadly — to explicitly cover sexual orientation and gender identity. It would also clarify that the Religious Freedom Restoration Act cannot be used as a defense to civil rights violations.

The Equality Act has passed the US House multiple times. It has stalled in the Senate. Constituent pressure on senators — particularly those not yet on record — is the operative lever. Letters from people who have lived in the protection gap (LGBTQ+ Americans denied housing, fired, refused service, removed from federally funded programs) carry particular weight.
