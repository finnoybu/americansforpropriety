The Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy provisions — production and investment tax credits for clean electricity, EV credits, building electrification incentives, the methane fee — are the largest US climate investment ever enacted. They have driven hundreds of billions in private investment in clean energy manufacturing and deployment, much of it in red and purple states (Georgia, Tennessee, Texas, Arizona, Michigan).

They are also targeted for repeal or weakening at every legislative opportunity. Tax-extender packages, debt-ceiling deals, end-of-year omnibus bills, and reconciliation vehicles have all been used to attempt clawbacks.

The political case for the credits has been changing. The investments they have triggered are now visible in many congressional districts as actual factories, jobs, and tax revenue. Members of Congress representing those districts have an immediate constituent interest in defending the credits, regardless of their general party position on climate policy.

A constituent letter that connects the IRA credits to **specific investments in your state or district** is one of the most effective things you can write on this issue. If you live near an IRA-funded facility — solar manufacturing, EV battery production, semiconductor fab — name it in your letter.
