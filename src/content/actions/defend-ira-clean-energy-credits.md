The Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy provisions — production and investment tax credits for clean electricity, building electrification incentives, manufacturing credits — were the largest US climate investment ever enacted. They drove hundreds of billions in private investment in clean energy manufacturing and deployment, much of it in red and purple states (Georgia, Tennessee, Texas, Arizona, Michigan).

Much of that package has now been rolled back. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025, ended the consumer EV tax credits on September 30, 2025, ends the residential clean-energy credit for homeowners on December 31, 2025, and sets a hard July 4, 2026 cutoff after which wind and solar projects beginning construction lose the production and investment tax credits. The repeal is no longer a pending threat — it is the law.

What is still live is the fight over the credits that survived — the technology-neutral credits for geothermal, storage, nuclear, and other generation — and over the clean-energy projects already in the pipeline. Every wind and solar project not yet under construction is now racing the July 4, 2026 deadline, and further rollbacks could chase down the credits that remain.

The political case for these credits is visible in many congressional districts as actual factories, jobs, and tax revenue — investment the OBBBA timelines now put at risk. Members of Congress representing those districts have an immediate constituent interest in defending what remains, regardless of their general party position on climate policy.

A constituent letter that connects the clean-energy credits to **specific investments in your state or district** is one of the most effective things you can write on this issue. If you live near a clean-energy facility — solar manufacturing, EV battery production, semiconductor fab — name it in your letter.
