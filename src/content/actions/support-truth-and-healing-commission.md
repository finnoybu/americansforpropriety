The Indian boarding school system, operating from the late 19th century into the late 20th century, was a federal policy designed to assimilate Native children by removing them from their families, languages, and cultures. The Department of the Interior's 2022-2024 investigation documented hundreds of federally funded boarding schools, the deaths of thousands of Native children at those schools, and the institutional patterns of physical, sexual, and emotional abuse that affected generations.

The Truth and Healing Commission on Indian Boarding School Policies Act would establish a federal commission to:

- Investigate the boarding school era comprehensively, including federal records that have not yet been fully made public
- Document survivor testimony and create a permanent historical record
- Identify burial sites and support tribal communities in addressing them
- Recommend specific federal actions for accountability, repair, and prevention
- Coordinate with parallel state-level and Canadian Indigenous-led work

The bill has been reintroduced in multiple Congresses. The political case rests on bipartisan recognition that the United States has not produced a comprehensive accounting of a federal policy that affected hundreds of thousands of Native families. Comparable commissions in Canada and Australia have produced substantial public records and policy responses; the US has not.

Letters to your senators and representatives — particularly those on Indian Affairs and Judiciary committees — push this up the legislative agenda. The bill's substantive case is well-established; the legislative bandwidth and political prioritization have been the constraint.

If you have family or community connection to the boarding school era, naming that in your letter is appropriate and powerful. If you're a constituent in a state with significant tribal population or with substantial historical involvement in the boarding school system, your senators have particular constituency interest.
