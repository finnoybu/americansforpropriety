The private equity playbook in healthcare, retail, and other sectors typically ends with a portfolio company bankruptcy that wipes out workers' pensions, severance, and back wages — while the PE owner has already extracted dividends, fees, and real-estate value during the hold period. The financial structure is designed so that the legal person who owes the workers (the portfolio company) has no money, and the legal person with the money (the PE fund) is shielded from the obligation.

The Stop Wall Street Looting Act would change this in several ways. Most importantly, it would establish **joint and several liability** for PE owners on portfolio company debts in bankruptcy — the legal architecture that lets workers reach the actual deep pockets when the immediate employer can't pay.

It also limits dividend recapitalizations during the early holding period, restricts certain sale-leaseback transactions, restores worker priority in bankruptcy, and requires PE firms to disclose ownership of healthcare and other regulated assets.

The bill has been reintroduced multiple times. Constituent pressure on senators — particularly those on the Banking and Finance committees — keeps it on the agenda.
