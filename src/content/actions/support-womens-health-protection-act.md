The Women's Health Protection Act would establish federal statutory protection for abortion access through fetal viability — codifying the standard that operated under Roe v. Wade from 1973-2022. The bill has passed the US House and stalled in the Senate, where the 60-vote cloture requirement has been the binding constraint.

The political case for federal codification has strengthened since Dobbs. Ballot measures protecting abortion access have passed in red and purple states (Kansas, Michigan, Ohio, Vermont, California, Kentucky, Arizona, Missouri, others). Polling on the substance of pre-Dobbs access shows large majorities, including in many states that have enacted abortion bans through their legislatures.

The Senate procedural fight has been the binding constraint. Each time the bill has had floor votes, it has produced position-clarifying votes from undecided senators. Constituent pressure on those senators — especially in states where ballot measures have shown majoritarian support for access — is the active lever.

If your senator is undecided or formally opposed, your letter is especially valuable. If your senator has voted for the WHPA, thanks are appropriate but not the highest-value advocacy. Letters that name specific local effects (clinics that have closed, travel patterns from your state, personal experience) carry weight.

State-level fights in your home state continue to matter even if your state has codified pre-Dobbs access in state law — federal action is the durable protection that makes state-level reversal less consequential.
