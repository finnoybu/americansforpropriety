The Better Care Better Jobs Act would provide substantial federal funding to expand Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) — the alternative to institutionalization for hundreds of thousands of disabled Americans and increasingly for older Americans needing long-term care.

The current pattern: more than 700,000 people are on HCBS waiting lists across 38 states. Many wait five to ten years or more. People die on these waiting lists. The federal-right-to-services architecture functions, in practice, as a federal-right-to-be-on-a-list.

The bill would address the structural cause — Medicaid's institutional bias built into federal funding architecture — by providing federal funding incentivizing state HCBS expansion. Specific provisions:

- Substantial federal HCBS funding ($150 billion+ over a decade in some versions)
- Direct-care worker wage standards conditioned on funding
- Mandatory state HCBS plans with waiting-list reduction targets
- Expanded supported-employment funding
- Money Follows the Person reauthorization at expanded scale

The Build Back Better framework included substantial HCBS investment that did not survive the Senate negotiations producing the Inflation Reduction Act. Reintroducing some version of the framework remains an active legislative priority.

Letters to senators and representatives are the operative lever. The political coalition for HCBS expansion has been growing — disability-rights organizations, senior advocacy groups, family-caregiver coalitions, and direct-care worker unions all support meaningful federal action. Your letter adds to the pressure that has gotten the bill this close to passage.

If you, a family member, or a person you provide care for is on a waiting list — or has been on one — naming that experience makes the policy concrete. If you're a direct-care worker, your perspective is especially valuable.
