Citizens United did not legalize unlimited *secret* political spending. It legalized unlimited corporate political spending and explicitly upheld disclosure regimes. The roughly half of federal political spending that now flows through dark-money channels does so through 501(c)(4) "social welfare" organizations and similar vehicles whose donor identities are not publicly reported.

The DISCLOSE Act would change this. It would require organizations spending over $10,000 on federal political activity to disclose donors above a threshold. It would apply disclosure to electioneering communications close to elections. It would require disclosure of major donors in political ads themselves. It would require government contractors to disclose political spending. It would pierce LLC structures used to launder donor identity.

The bill survives constitutional scrutiny because it is, in legal terms, a disclosure regime, not a contribution or expenditure cap. The Supreme Court has consistently distinguished disclosure from caps. The political opposition is from donors who currently use dark-money channels precisely to avoid public accountability — not from anyone making a serious First Amendment argument.

The DISCLOSE Act has been introduced in every Congress since 2010. It came closest to passage in 2022 (failed cloture in the Senate). Constituent pressure on senators — particularly those who have not yet cosponsored — is the most direct lever.
