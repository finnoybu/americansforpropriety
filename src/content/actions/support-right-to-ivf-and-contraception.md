The 2022 Dobbs ruling did not directly address contraception or IVF. State-level fetal-personhood frameworks have brought both into legal pressure. The 2024 Alabama ruling treating embryos as "extrauterine children" temporarily halted IVF treatment in the state. Justice Thomas's Dobbs concurrence explicitly invited reconsideration of Griswold v. Connecticut, the 1965 ruling protecting contraception access.

Two federal protective bills have advanced in response:

**Right to IVF Act.** Federal statutory protection for IVF treatment regardless of state-level personhood frameworks. Includes federal funding support and insurance coverage requirements. Has had Senate floor votes; not yet enacted.

**Right to Contraception Act.** Federal protection for access to contraception, including IUDs, emergency contraception, and hormonal contraception. Has had Senate floor votes; not yet enacted.

Both bills have produced position-clarifying votes from senators who have been uncomfortable taking public positions on IVF and contraception specifically. Polling on both shows large majorities — including majorities of self-identified pro-life voters on IVF — that exceed support for many other reproductive-rights provisions.

The political case is unusually clean. Constituent letters, particularly from senators on the fence, have substantial leverage. Letters from people who have benefited from IVF treatment, or from people who depend on contraception for medical or family-planning reasons, name the policy concretely.

If your representative or senator has voted for either bill, thanks are appropriate but not the highest-value advocacy. If they've voted against or have been on the fence, your letter is especially valuable.
