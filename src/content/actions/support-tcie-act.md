Section 14(c) of the Fair Labor Standards Act, enacted in 1938, still authorizes the Secretary of Labor to issue special certificates allowing payment of disabled workers below the federal minimum wage. Workers employed under 14(c) certificates earn, on average, well below the federal minimum wage; some earn pennies per hour.

The framework does not, on the empirical record, function as a bridge to mainstream employment. It functions as a permanent destination — segregated workshop settings that workers rarely leave. State-level phase-outs in Maine, New Hampshire, Maryland, Vermont, and others have demonstrated that affected workers transition successfully to competitive integrated employment when supported-employment infrastructure is funded.

The Transformation to Competitive Integrated Employment Act would phase out 14(c) certificates over a defined transition period:

- Five-to-seven-year phase-out timeline for new certificates and existing renewals
- Federal funding for transition support (workshops converting their operations or closing; individual workers transitioning to competitive employment)
- Expanded supported-employment services
- Conversion grants for workshops moving to integrated-employment business models
- Worker-protection provisions during the transition

The bill has had bipartisan support in multiple sessions. Republican and Democratic cosponsors. Disability-rights organizations and employer-advocacy groups have engaged in negotiation. The legislative bandwidth competing with other priorities has been the constraint.

Letters to your senators and representative on Education and Workforce committees, HELP committees, and Health/Education Appropriations subcommittees move this up the legislative agenda.

If you, a family member, or someone you know has worked under 14(c) — or has transitioned out of workshop employment to competitive employment — naming that experience makes the policy concrete.
