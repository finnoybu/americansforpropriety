The 2022 PACT Act expanded VA toxic-exposure benefits to hundreds of thousands of veterans. Two years into implementation, the substantive framework has functioned as designed. The operational pressure points — claims backlogs, healthcare capacity, appeals queues — require sustained appropriations to address.

Annual VA appropriations determine staffing levels at both the Veterans Benefits Administration (claims processing, appeals) and Veterans Health Administration (clinical care). Recent years have included substantial increases; sustained funding is required to avoid backsliding.

Letters to your senators and representatives — particularly those on Veterans Affairs Committees and Military Construction-VA Appropriations subcommittees — push annual VA appropriations toward levels that match implementation needs. Congressional VA staff have substantial influence on appropriation levels; sustained constituent pressure is the most reliable lever.

Specific advocacy targets:

- VBA claims-processing staffing to clear backlogs
- VHA staffing to maintain wait-time and access-quality standards in high-demand regions
- Continued exposure-link expansion (additional presumptive conditions as scientific evidence accumulates)
- VA infrastructure investment (many VA facilities are decades old and operating beyond planned lifespans)
- Camp Lejeune litigation administrative support to manage the substantial federal-court workload

If you're a veteran, family member, or VA employee, your perspective has particular weight. If you have a specific PACT Act claim experience — successful or stalled — naming it in your letter makes the policy concrete. If you live near a VA facility under capacity pressure, naming it is similarly useful.
