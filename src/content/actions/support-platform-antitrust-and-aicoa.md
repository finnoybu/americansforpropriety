US antitrust enforcement against major technology platforms is the most active in a generation. The DOJ's 2024 victory in the Google search case was the most significant antitrust ruling against a US technology company in decades. The remedies fight will determine whether the substantive ruling produces structural change.

Several parallel cases — DOJ v. Google (AdTech), FTC v. Meta, DOJ v. Apple, FTC v. Amazon — are at various stages. Each has potential structural-remedy implications. The combined effect, should the cases produce structural remedies, would be the most significant restructuring of US technology industries since the 1980s AT&T breakup.

The legislative complement is the American Innovation and Choice Online Act and the Open App Markets Act. AICOA prohibits specific anticompetitive practices by dominant platforms — self-preferencing, anti-steering, certain forms of discrimination — without requiring antitrust litigation in each instance. The bill has been reintroduced in multiple sessions; it has not been enacted.

Letters to senators and representatives — particularly those on Judiciary committees and antitrust subcommittees — push AICOA up the legislative agenda. The substantive case is well-established; the political coalition has been bipartisan in principle but has faced industry pressure that has prevented advancement.

Two specific advocacy targets:

**Defend structural remedies.** The DOJ has signaled support for structural remedies in the Google search case (Chrome divestiture, Android divestiture, default-payment prohibition). Letters supporting structural rather than conduct remedies push the political environment toward the more durable outcome.

**Support AICOA reintroduction and floor consideration.** Senate procedural posture has been the binding constraint. Constituent letters to undecided senators are the active lever.

If you're a small business that sells through dominant platforms, an app developer affected by App Store policies, or a content creator whose distribution depends on platforms, your perspective is particularly useful. The political conversation often centers on consumer effects; the small-business and creator-side effects are equally substantive but less visible.
