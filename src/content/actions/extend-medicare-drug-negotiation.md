The Inflation Reduction Act gave Medicare the authority to negotiate prices for selected high-cost drugs. The first round of negotiations completed in 2024 produced price reductions of 38% to 79% on ten widely used drugs — saving Medicare and seniors billions and setting prices that go into effect in 2026.

The next rounds expand the list each year. But the program is narrow by design: Medicare only (not private insurance), with a long market-exclusivity window before drugs become eligible for negotiation. Multiple expansions are on the table:

- Expand the eligible drug list beyond the current cap.
- Shorten the market-exclusivity window before negotiation eligibility.
- Extend negotiated prices to private insurance.
- Defend the program against repeal proposals.

The pharmaceutical industry has spent heavily to weaken the program in subsequent legislation and through litigation. Constituent letters from people whose families benefit from negotiated prices — or from people facing high drug costs — are particularly effective on senators who are not yet committed.

If your representative or senator is on the Finance Committee, the Health Subcommittee, or HELP Committee, your letter has additional weight.
