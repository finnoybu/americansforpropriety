State budgets are where most policy actually happens. They're also where most policy goes unwitnessed — committee rooms are usually empty, and the lobbyists who do show up have a near-monopoly on the public record.

Written testimony is one of the most undervalued tools available to a constituent. You don't need a lawyer. You don't need to attend a hearing. You need to identify the relevant committee, find the testimony submission form (almost always on the legislature's website), and write 200–500 words explaining how a specific budget line affects your community.

A good testimony does three things: identifies the bill or budget line, explains the concrete local effect, and makes a specific ask.
