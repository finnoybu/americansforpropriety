State legislatures are the operative venue for most US insurance regulation under the McCarran-Ferguson Act. As private insurers withdraw from California wildfire zones, Florida hurricane corridors, Louisiana flood zones, and others, state legislatures are working to reform insurance markets, fund state insurers of last resort, and (in some cases) impose climate-resilience requirements on new construction.

The legislative committees doing this work — typically state insurance committees, banking committees, and natural-resources committees — accept written testimony on most relevant bills. The format expectations are minimal. You don't need a lawyer, a lobbyist, or even an in-person appearance. The submission process is usually a form on the legislature's website.

If you are a homeowner in an affected zone, a small business owner in an exposed industry, or a renter facing rising costs from insurance pass-through — your testimony has weight. Specific local impact (your premium, your neighbor's, the property values on your block) is more useful than abstract argument.

Several state legislatures are also debating climate-resilient building codes, managed retreat programs, and state climate-disclosure rules for businesses. All of these are venues where a few minutes of writing produces durable public record.
