Our last recap closed on May 9, with the shutdown over and reconciliation 2.0 still moving. Six months later the political map looks different. Republicans used the summer to pass one more party-line bill. The Supreme Court finished a term that reshaped presidential power. The Iran war ground on under a ceasefire that Congress never authorized. On November 3, voters ended the Republican trifecta.

## Reconciliation 2.0 becomes law

The Senate passed the second reconciliation bill on **June 5** after an 18-hour vote-a-rama, and the House cleared it **June 9** on a party-line 214-212 vote. The president signed it shortly after. The bill provides roughly $70 billion for ICE and CBP, money that Congress had deliberately left out of the regular DHS appropriations bill that ended the spring shutdown. ([NLIHC](https://nlihc.org/resource/house-republicans-pass-and-president-signs-law-reconciliation-20-providing-70-billion-ice))

## Congress passes an Iran war powers measure

The House passed a resolution on **June 3** to block further strikes on Iran without congressional approval, the first time under Trump that either chamber had used its war powers authority this way. On **June 24**, after the tenth Senate Democratic attempt since the war began, Congress passed an Iran war powers measure for the first time. The same day, the Pentagon asked for about $80 billion more, most of it for the war. ([Washington Post via OBRAG](https://obrag.org/2026/06/for-first-time-under-trump-congress-exerts-its-war-powers-authority-house-passes-bill-to-block-further-strikes-on-iran/), [People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/news/congress-passes-war-powers-measure-for-first-time-rebuking-trumps-war-with-iran/amp))

The 60-day War Powers deadline had passed on May 1. The administration's position remains that hostilities "terminated" with the April ceasefire, even as the naval blockade continued.

## The Supreme Court's term ends

The Court's final weeks produced a cluster of rulings that will define the rest of this administration ([Connecticut Public / NPR](https://www.ctpublic.org/2026-06-09/here-are-the-major-supreme-court-decisions-decided-this-term)):

- **Birthright citizenship** (*Trump v. Barbara*, **June 30**, 6-3): the Constitution guarantees citizenship to virtually all children born in the US. The executive order is dead.
- **Agency independence** (*Trump v. Slaughter*, **June 29**, 6-3): the president lawfully fired an FTC commissioner, a major expansion of removal power over independent agencies.
- **The Fed** (*Trump v. Cook*, **June 29**, 5-4): Governor Lisa Cook keeps her seat for now.
- **Mail ballots** (*Watson v. RNC*, **June 29**, 5-4): states may count mail ballots postmarked by Election Day and received a few days later.
- **TPS** (*Mullin v. Doe*, **June 25**, 6-3): the president has essentially unreviewable authority to end Temporary Protected Status.
- **Transgender athletes** (*Little v. Hecox* / *West Virginia v. B.P.J.*, **June 30**): states may bar transgender girls from girls' sports at public schools.
- **Geofence warrants** (*Chatrie v. US*, **June 29**, 6-3): the Court restricted them and sent the case back.

## A stopgap through December 11

With no FY2027 appropriations bills finished, Congress passed a continuing resolution, and the president signed it on **September 2** (P.L. 119-103). It funds the government at FY2026 levels from October 1 through **December 11**. The outgoing Congress now has a lame-duck funding deadline before the new majorities take over. ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-17_R49353_98ddd8e7b1852b47b24e394ed7ddf6d6d53b4632.html))

## November 3: Democrats take both chambers

**The Senate.** Democrats won control with at least 54 seats, flipping Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina, and Texas, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled at last count. ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/2026-Midterm-Election-Guide-to-the-120th-Congress-11-4-2026)) James Talarico's win in Texas was the party's first Senate victory there since 1988. ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/international/us-midterms-2026-list-of-winners-as-democrats-flip-senate-dent-trumps-grip))

**The House.** Democrats needed five seats and won far more: at least 33 pickups, with the new majority likely to exceed 250 members once counting finishes. ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/2026-Midterm-Election-Guide-to-the-120th-Congress-11-4-2026)) Several Florida seats that Republicans had redrawn in their favor flipped anyway. ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=4791174))

**Ballot measures.** Results were mixed, but voters guarded their own power. Missouri voters rejected the legislature's attempt to repeal the 2024 abortion-rights amendment and threw out the 2025 Republican congressional map 62-38. Nevada and Virginia passed abortion-rights amendments; Idaho's failed. Voter ID passed in Nevada, North Carolina, and Ohio and failed in California and Colorado. Virginia voters restored voting rights to people leaving prison, and Michigan and Montana passed campaign finance measures by wide margins. North Dakota, South Dakota, and Utah rejected measures making it harder to pass citizen initiatives. ([State Court Report](https://statecourtreport.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/what-2026-ballot-measure-results-tell-us))

## The leadership races

**House.** Hakeem Jeffries formally launched his bid for Speaker on **November 4**. House Democrats vote on their nominee behind closed doors on **November 18**; the full House elects the Speaker when the new Congress convenes in January. ([ABC News](https://abc7news.com/live-updates/midterm-elections-2026-live-updates-election-results-key-races-us/20001292/entry/20016171/)) On the Republican side, Mike Johnson said he will not run for minority leader, and Steve Scalise and Jim Jordan are competing for the job. ([ABC News](https://abc7news.com/live-updates/midterm-elections-2026-live-updates-election-results-key-races-us/20001292/entry/20015686/))

**Senate.** Chuck Schumer says he will be re-elected leader. Senate Democrats are expected to vote in early December by secret ballot, with the senators-elect taking part. Several newly elected senators, including Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota, said during their campaigns that it was time for new leadership. ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/04/will-be-reelected-as-majority-leader-schumer-says/))

## What the new majority says it will do

House Democrats have said they will move first on affordability: restoring the ACA premium tax credits that expired in 2025, reversing the Medicaid cuts in the 2025 reconciliation law, reversing the tariffs, and forcing more votes to end the Iran war. They plan to start in the first 100 hours of the new Congress. ([AP via ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/wireStory/house-democrats-plan-100-days-agenda-sweep-power-137247631))

Majorities do not end vetoes. Anything that reaches the president's desk without a two-thirds margin can still be blocked, and the filibuster remains a choice the new Senate majority will have to make. What changes on January 3 is who sets the agenda, who runs the committees, and who has subpoena power.

## What this period produced

The spring ended with the fights intensified. The fall ended with a verdict on them. Voters were asked, in effect, whether two years of unchecked executive power, an unauthorized war, and rising costs were acceptable, and the answer was no, in red states as well as blue ones.

A verdict is not a program. The next two years will test whether the new majorities can turn a rebuke into results, and whether the institutions that bent under pressure this year can be rebuilt to hold.
